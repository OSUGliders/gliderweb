ARCTERX

In the Western Pacific, the combination of the Kuroshio Current, the monsoon, strong tides, and the dramatic topography create a rich soup of ocean features, such as eddies and filaments. The Island Arc Turbulent Eddy Regional Exchange (ARCTERX) DRI is using a fleet of autonomous vehicles, models, and remote sensing products to better quantify interactions between features spanning scales from 1 - 100 km. Through this effort, Glider-Research-Group-supported Slocum gliders and turbulence sensors are uncovering the complex mechanisms dissipating ocean energy.

Washington Coast Monitoring

Deploying autonomous underwater gliders off the Washington Coast to monitor critical oceanographic parameters. This initiative helps track seasonal upwelling, hypoxia events, and ecosystem health in the California Current System.

RIOT

Interior submesoscale currents (1 - 10 km), occurring below the ocean mixed layer, are thought to facilitate heat transport and pollutant dispersal, but are difficult to observe. The Glider Research Group is working to develop a new underwater sensing network to study interior currents for the Researching Interior Ocean Trajectories (RIOT) DRI. Our Slocum gliders will become an integral component of a large underwater network of autonomous underwater robots. The robotic network will provide a unique view on the interior submesoscale.
